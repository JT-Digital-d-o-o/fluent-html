# Juxtaposition — RFC-B-010 vs. Wave-3 verdicts vs. shipped code

RFC-B-010 (native Popover API, invoker Commands, CSS anchor positioning) is **not implemented** — no `setPopover` / `setPopovertarget` / `setCommand` / `anchorName` / `positionAnchor` / `positionArea`, no `PopoverState`/`PopoverAction`/`CommandFor`/`TailwindPositionArea`, and no anchor vocab rows exist anywhere in `src/`. Every `required_change` below is therefore a `must-apply-when-built` directive — a fix to fold into the RFC/implementation when it lands. Each verdict claim was independently verified against the actual shipped code; results noted in Evidence.

## Notes

- No `killer_objection` in any of the three lenses — all verdicts are `survives-with-changes`. No finding rises to a kill.
- The **popover** and **command** halves are clean across all lenses (closed unions, schema-key serialization through `escapeAttr`, `openDialog`/`closeDialog`/`BooleanAttribute` genuinely untouched — `BooleanAttribute` confirmed closed at html-types.ts:58-63). The substantive defects all cluster on the **anchor-positioning** third: the Id↔string parity bridge (changes #1/#6, high), the false ID-safety claim (#2/#4), and the misrepresented static-extraction story (#3, high).
- Two pairs of changes are the same defect seen from two lenses: correctness#1 ≡ type-safety#6 (parity bridge); correctness#2 ≡ security#4 (false `defineIds` safety claim). When building, one fix per pair satisfies both.
