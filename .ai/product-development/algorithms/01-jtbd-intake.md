# Algorithm 01: JTBD Intake

Turn a raw idea into a durable anchor: the **Job** the customer is hiring the product to do,
in plain language. Everything downstream (research, validation, naming, scope) reads this
first. Grounded in [Competing Against Luck](../resources/competing-against-luck/jobs-to-be-done.md).

- **Input:** a raw product idea, and access to at least one real person in the target
  circumstance (for the interview step).
- **Output:** a filled `vision.md` (sections 1 to 14 below), plus notes from 1 to 3
  switch-moment interviews.
- **Done when:** every `✍️` is replaced or explicitly left blank, the Job fits in one job
  story, the Four Forces are all named, and the anti-job is written. A reviewer who has never
  seen the product can restate the Job in one sentence.

> **Describe the Job, do not design the product.** Write the progress the customer is trying
> to make and the struggle they are in. Later phases work out which features actually do that
> Job. Features are guesses; the Job is durable.

---

## Steps

1. **Draft the vision solo.** The person closest to the idea fills the template below in
   plain language. Leave blanks where unsure; a flagged unknown beats a confident guess.
2. **Interview 1 to 3 real people** in the target circumstance using the switch-moment
   prompts. Do not ask what features they want; they will describe solutions, not Jobs. Go
   upstream from every feature request to the circumstance that created it.
3. **Rewrite the vision** against what you heard. The struggling moment (§1), what they hire
   today (§6), and the Four Forces (§8) are the sections interviews most often correct.
4. **Gate:** a second person restates the Job from the doc alone. If they can't, it isn't
   ready to anchor the next phase.

---

## The template (copy this into `vision.md`)

> Product / working name: `✍️ …`
> Author: `✍️ …`  ·  Date: `✍️ …`
> Who this is for (one line): `✍️ …`

### 1. The struggling moment (the circumstance)
The exact moment the customer realises "I have a problem." What just happened, where are
they, what triggered it? A Job lives in a situation, not in a person. Include location or
jurisdiction if it changes the Job.

> `✍️ …`

### 2. The Job, in one line (job-story format)
Not a user story. Fill the blanks:

> **"When** `✍️ situation` **, I want to** `✍️ motivation` **, so I can** `✍️ outcome` **."**

### 3. The functional job: what literally must get done
The practical task, in the customer's words.

> `✍️ …`

### 4. The emotional job: how they want to feel
Which feeling are they chasing, and which are they escaping? Most people hire a tool to
**stop feeling** something as much as to gain something.

> Want to feel: `✍️ …`
> Want to STOP feeling: `✍️ …`

### 5. The social job: how they want to be seen
By whom, and as what?

> `✍️ …`

### 6. What do they hire today? (and why they would fire it)
Every Job is already done somehow: a rival, a manual workaround, an intern, a spreadsheet, or
doing nothing. List each and what is wrong with it.

> Hire today: `✍️ …`
> Why they would fire it: `✍️ …`

### 7. The real competitor might be "nothing"
Is the true rival another product, or **inertia** (keep coping by hand, do without, accept the
risk)? For most products the largest competitor is non-consumption. Name who you displace.

> `✍️ …`

### 8. The Four Forces (what makes them switch, or not)
Switch happens only when Push + Pull overpower Anxiety + Habit. Anxiety and habit are the two
most underestimated.

> - **Push** (what makes the old way intolerable): `✍️ …`
> - **Pull** (what attracts them to the new way): `✍️ …`
> - **Anxiety** (what worries them about switching to us): `✍️ …`
> - **Habit** (what keeps them comfortable now): `✍️ …`

### 9. "Done well": the customer's own yardstick
How does the customer measure that the Job was nailed? Their metric, not ours. Be concrete.

> `✍️ …`

### 10. The hiring criteria: what tips the choice
At the decision moment, what proof, feeling, or guarantee makes them pick us over the
alternative (including over doing nothing)?

> `✍️ …`

### 11. Feature hunches (hypotheses, not specs)
Dump ideas freely, framed as bets on doing the Job better, not requirements. Later phases
rate each against the Job, feasibility, and market or regulation.

> - `✍️ …`

### 12. Non-negotiables
What must always be true for this to do the Job at all?

> - `✍️ …`

### 13. Anti-job: what this is deliberately NOT
Jobs you will not take on. Keeps scope honest and protects positioning.

> - `✍️ …`

### 14. Open questions and anything else
Mood, brand feeling, a real customer in mind, pricing instinct, anything with no section above.

> `✍️ …`

## Appendix: Switch-moment interview prompts

1. What were you doing when you first realised you needed something like this?
2. What did you try before, and why was it not good enough?
3. Walk me through the moment you decided to try a new way.
4. What were you worried would happen if it did not work?
5. When you last did this the old way, what specifically made it painful?
6. Who else notices whether you get this right, and what do they think?

## Nudges

- **DO** segment by circumstance, not demographics. "Someone asked to present in 30 minutes
  without their data ready" beats "Sarah, 34, marketing manager."
- **DO** capture all three dimensions (functional, social, emotional). Loyalty and switching
  costs come from nailing all three, not just the functional one.
- **DON'T** accept a job story that describes a solution ("When on the dashboard, I want to
  click Export"). Push upstream to the circumstance and the emotional stakes.
- **DON'T** treat §11 hunches as a spec. They are bets, tested in Algorithm 02 and Validation.
